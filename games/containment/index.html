How to play

Your job

A new pathogen has been confirmed in one region. You lead the world's response. You win when there is not a single active infection left anywhere. You lose if the death toll passes the limit the world can absorb, or if public trust collapses so far that governments stop following you.

What you can see

The map shows confirmed cases, not real ones. Poor surveillance, silent carriers and weak health systems hide most of the epidemic, especially early on. A region looks clean until it confirms its first cases. Detection upgrades close the gap, and Epidemic modelling reveals the true picture.

Funding and Authority

  • Funding trickles in from donor nations and pays for upgrades in Response. A weak economy or low trust means less of it.
  • Authority is political capital. You spend it to impose measures in a region. It regenerates faster while people trust you.
  • Tap the bubbles that appear on the map: gold is funding, blue is a research breakthrough, orange is outbreak intelligence that also improves detection, and violet is Authority.

Measures

Tap a region to open it and impose measures: testing, tracing, masks, distancing, school closures, lockdowns, border, port and flight closures, field hospitals and, later, vaccination drives. Every measure slows the spread, but the harsher ones cost economy and patience every day they stay in force. Tired publics comply less, and a public that loses all trust defies you outright. Restrictions in a region with few visible cases cost more trust, because people cannot see why.

Upgrades

Open Response to fund upgrades in five areas: Detect, Contain, Treat, Vaccine and Society. Each one unlocks the next. Prices rise a little with every upgrade you buy, so you will not get everything.

The vaccine

Research needs the pathogen's genome first: buy Genome sequencing early. Research runs in the labs of wealthy regions, so wrecking their economies slows it. Once approved, doses are limited by factories, by how fast each region can vaccinate, by who trusts the vaccine, and by rich regions buying first unless you fund equitable allocation.

The pathogen fights back

Every infection is a chance to mutate. The pathogen earns evolution from the number of people infected, and it spends it on whatever counters your strategy: silent spread if you rely on testing, faster transmission if you rely on restrictions, immune escape once you vaccinate, drug resistance once you treat. Keep infections low and it evolves slowly. Genomic surveillance spots new variants early.

Controls

  • Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Tap a region to open it.
  • Space pauses, 1 2 3 set the speed, R opens Response, W opens World, Esc closes panels.
